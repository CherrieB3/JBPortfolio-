TEAM PROJECT • UX RESEARCH & INTERACTION DESIGN • MAY 2025

🚗 Comet Commute

A concept for reducing parking stress at UT Dallas by bringing real-time availability and predictive suggestions into the campus app students already use.

Role
UX Researcher & Interaction Designer
Team
Team project
Timeline
May 2025
Methods
User interviews, personas, prototyping

Overview

Using research, ideation, and prototyping, my team and I designed a concept to improve the commuting and parking experience at UT Dallas. We focused on making parking more predictable and efficient by integrating real-time availability and class-schedule-based suggestions into the existing UTD app — aiming to reduce student frustration, streamline commute planning, and increase the perceived value of parking passes.

The Problem

Finding parking at UTD was a persistent source of stress for both local and commuting students. Between limited available spots, no way to account for parking time within a commute, and a widespread feeling that parking passes weren't worth their cost, the existing system left students guessing every morning.

Key problems identified

  • Difficulty finding convenient, available parking
  • Uncertainty about parking time affecting on-time arrival to class
  • Low perceived value of the parking pass itself

Research & Personas

We interviewed students who lived on campus and students who commuted long distances, and reviewed existing campus parking studies to understand behaviors and pain points across both groups.

🚙 The Chill Local Student

Short commute, relaxed about timing — mainly frustrated by the semester-start parking crunch.

🛣️ The Frustrated Distance Commuter

Long commute, high parking stress, and real frustration with cost and availability.

Approach & Key Decisions

We weighed three ways to close the gap between students and open spots:

Pressure plates

New hardware in every lot — high cost, slow rollout.

Standalone app

Full control, but asks students to adopt yet another app.

Honor-system check-in

Cheap, but relies on manual reporting that goes stale fast.

We chose to build into the UTD app students already use, and drive adoption through orientation — meeting students where they already were.

Final Solution

Our concept enhanced the UTD app with three core features:

  1. Real-time availability — detects open parking spots as they happen
  2. Smart lot suggestions — recommends lots based on distance, availability, and walking time to class
  3. Predictive planning — uses data collected over time to forecast busy periods so students can plan ahead

Reflection

Adoption is a design problem in its own right — building into a system students already use, rather than around it, is what made this concept feel achievable.
